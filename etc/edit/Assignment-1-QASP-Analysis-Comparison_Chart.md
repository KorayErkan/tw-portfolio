### Analysis Summary

The three QASP documents share a common objective: to provide a systematic method for the government to ensure a contractor meets the required performance standards. However, they demonstrate significant variations in their specific approaches and structural components.

* **U.S. Army Medical Command** and the **IRS** both emphasize their QASPs as tools for monitoring performance against a PWS and ensuring payment for only acceptable services. The GSA's approach is more focused on the **performance-based** nature of the acquisition, providing a framework for the "what" rather than the "how."
* All three documents delineate clear roles and responsibilities for government personnel, such as the Contracting Officer and their representatives, to ensure proper oversight.
* While all three mention a variety of surveillance methods (e.g., 100% inspection, random sampling), the **IRS** document provides a more extensive list including **third-party audits** and decision tables to resolve disputes. The **GSA** template emphasizes the use of specific forms and reports to document the surveillance process.

Overall, the documents illustrate that while the fundamental principles of a QASP remain consistent, the specific implementation can be tailored to the unique needs of different agencies and contract types.
