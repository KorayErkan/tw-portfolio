1.1. Machine Overview

  2. Way covers — inspect for tears/binding; verify smooth travel across full stroke.

  3. Hydraulic unit — replace filter per schedule; verify pressure within spec at idle and load.

  4. Coolant tank and chip conveyor — clean intake screen; check return baffles; maintain 6–8% concentration.

  5. Emergency stop (E-Stop) — on the operator panel; see Emergency Shutdown Procedures.

2. Safety Requirements

Maintenance must be performed only by trained personnel. Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) is mandatory before opening guards or working near moving parts. Failure to follow these instructions may result in serious injury.

2.1. PPE (Personal Protective Equipment)

  • Safety helmet and ANSI Z87.1/EN 166 eye protection

  • Cut-resistant gloves appropriate to task

  • Hearing protection (≥ 25 dB SNR)

  • Steel-toe safety footwear

  • Protective clothing free of loose items (no jewelry, secure long hair)

2.2. Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) — Summary

  1. Notify affected operators and stop the machine; press E-Stop.

  2. Isolate electrical energy: set main disconnect to OFF; apply personal lock and tag.

  3. Isolate pneumatic/hydraulic energy: close supply valves; bleed off stored pressure; apply locks/tags.

  4. Isolate mechanical/kinetic energy: block or brace moving parts as required.

  5. Verify zero energy:

    1. Attempt a normal start to confirm isolation (machine must not power).

    2. Use appropriate test devices: absence-of-voltage indicator; gauges at 0 pressure.

  6. Perform maintenance.

  7. Remove tools/fixtures, reinstall guards, clear work area.

  8. Remove personal locks/tags; restore energy in reverse order; perform a controlled test run.

Figure 2. Energy isolation points, locked out

  1. Main electrical disconnect — rotated to OFF.

  2. Personal padlock and blank tag — write your name, date, and reason on the tag.

  3. Pneumatic supply ball valve — closed, with valve lockout device and padlock.

Follow facility-specific procedures in addition to this summary. Reference: OSHA 1910.147 (Control of Hazardous Energy), ISO 16090-1 (Machine tool safety), and applicable local regulations.

2.3. Emergency Shutdown Procedures

2.3.1. Electrical Emergency

  1. Press E-Stop immediately — red mushroom button on control panel and pendant

  2. Turn main disconnect to OFF — located at electrical cabinet

  3. Do not attempt to restart until hazard is eliminated

  4. Call maintenance supervisor and facility emergency response team

  5. Evacuate 3-meter radius if sparking, smoke, or unusual odors present

2.3.2. Mechanical Emergency (Collision, Jam, Broken Tool)

  1. Press E-Stop — do not attempt to clear obstruction while powered

  2. Assess for injury — call medical emergency if personnel involved

  3. Isolate all energy sources following full LOTO procedure

  4. Document damage with photos before disturbing evidence

  5. Contact OEM service for spindle impact assessment if collision occurred

2.3.3. Hydraulic/Coolant Leak

  1. Stop machine immediately — E-Stop if leak is active/spraying

  2. Contain spill with absorbent materials and drain pans

  3. Ventilate area — hydraulic fluid vapors can be hazardous

  4. Check for slip hazards — barricade wet areas immediately

  5. Report to environmental safety for disposal of contaminated absorbents

3.1. Service Points

Figure 3. Spindle lubrication points (weekly)

  • A — upper bearing housing fitting.

  • B — front bearing housing fitting.

Apply 2–3 pumps of NLGI-2 grease per fitting with the spindle stopped and locked out. Do not over-pack: excess grease raises bearing temperature.

Figure 4. Hydraulic power unit (monthly filter change)

  1. Return-line filter housing — replace the element monthly.

  2. Differential-pressure (ΔP) indicator — log the reading before and after the filter change.

  3. System pressure gauge — verify ≥ 42 bar at idle (see fault E-301).

  4. Oil-level sight glass — keep level above the 75% mark.

  5. Reservoir drain valve — use for oil sampling and complete drain-down.

4.1. After-Clear Checklist

  1. Clear alarms; home all axes.

  2. Run a short warm-up cycle (spindle + axes); observe temperatures and loads.

  3. Verify part accuracy on a test coupon before resuming production.

Do not clear and restart repeatedly without investigation. Recurrent or intermittent faults may indicate wiring damage, failing sensors, or drive issues.

4.2. Escalate to OEM/Service When

  • Spindle temperature rises rapidly at idle or faults recur after cooldown.

  • Servo faults persist after verifying cables, connectors, and supply voltages.

  • Hydraulic pressure fluctuates under steady load or ΔP gauge spikes after filter change.

5.2. Usage Rules

  • Complete entries in permanent ink; no erasures. Use single strike-through for corrections and add initials/date.

  • Record machine hours at the start of each task.

  • Note torque values, measurements, and test results (e.g., coolant 7.2% Brix, backlash 0.006 mm).

  • Attach supporting checklists (E-Stop/door interlock tests) and reference their file IDs in the log.

  • After any safety or motion-related maintenance, run a controlled test program and record results.

5.3. Retention

Maintain maintenance logs and checklists for 5 years (or per facility policy). Provide on request to auditors or OEM service.
